Overview

Turning idle waiting into the first moment of the visit

The problem

The elevator bottleneck

The Lipsticks Lookout has limited elevator capacity, which creates a natural gathering point on the ground floor. Today that pause is filled with static murals visitors walk past.

How do we turn idle waiting into a memorable entry point?

The solution

Step up, take a photo, meet your historical double

A kiosk takes your portrait. A generative model reimagines you as an 18th or 19th-century merchant, coachman or noble, and your double joins a living mural on the wall while you wait for the lift.

The pivot

We started with a 3D mirror. It didn’t work.

Our first concept tracked the visitor’s body and moved a matching 3D historical avatar through a digital reconstruction of the square. Early prototype tests ruled it out.

1

The visuals didn’t match

Three.js couldn’t reproduce the lighting and textures built in Blender, so the scene looked distorted.

2

The bodies looked wrong

Tracking data made limbs clip through the meshes, and flat 2D faces on rigid 3D bodies broke the feeling of “that’s me”.

3

It was too slow

Live 3D rendering plus body tracking caused delays, the opposite of shortening the wait.

So we pivoted

To a high-fidelity 2D generative workflow in ComfyUI. Before finalising the interface we ran a full-pipeline feasibility test, covering capture, generation and display in one staging website.

The interface

Designing the kiosk

We mapped every tap a visitor would make, from the moment the kiosk wakes up to the second they spot themselves on the wall, and prototyped each screen in Figma.

The selfie and confirm screens are left out here because they show a placeholder portrait. Confirm or retake gives visitors an undo before generation starts.

We cut the tutorial

Testing showed the kiosk was readable without one.

Three roles, not six

The project’s time limit forced the choice. We’d want to grow it.

Two screens

Citizens that stay

While you play, a second process turns your photo into a generic historical citizen from one of ten prompts. After you leave, that citizen stays in the scene.

Design principle · playful urban interaction

Generative AI lets people playfully alter the urban scene instead of passively consuming it. Gothenburg’s history becomes a place visitors briefly inhabit, not a panel they read.

After Hung et al., 2025, CHI.

Worth knowing, from our report

Survey bias. Engaged visitors stayed to talk and were more likely to fill in the survey, so the ratings may be higher than for everyone who tried it.

Not everyone likes it. One visitor said they dislike AI changing their picture, whatever the quality.

AI can surprise. During testing, one generated character was missing clothing on the lower body, which was awkward for the visitor and their family. We strengthened the prompts straight away. A public installation would need more safeguards.

Looking back and forward

What the pilot said, and what the exhibition showed

Before: the pilot

A small pilot said the experience was fun but the wait felt long, so we weighed two paths.

Path A · Make the wait shorter

Move generation to cloud hardware.

Path B · Make the wait fun

Fill the wait with quizzes and fun facts about Gothenburg.

After: the exhibition

At the exhibition only 3 of 33 visitors found the wait too long, and 7 of 33 picked quizzes as their top improvement. More roles (13 of 33) and a better likeness mattered more. Together this suggests the wait was no longer the main problem.

My reading of the survey, not a team decision.

If we kept going

  • Add more roles and characters, the most requested change.
  • Improve how much the character looks like the visitor.
  • Add more scenes, and let visitors choose their own context.
  • Test the setup: an on-site kiosk or iPad against visitors using their own phones.
  • Shorten generation time and add quizzes or fun facts to the wait.
  • Add safeguards for unpredictable AI output before any public deployment.

Reflection

What I took away

1

A constraint can be the brief

The limited lift capacity became the whole experience, and 91% found the wait fine.

2

Delight carries a rough prototype

Visitors laughed at imperfect results, but likeness was the weakest score at 3.36 out of 5.

3

Test in the real place

The exhibition showed what the pilot didn’t: the wait wasn’t the problem, the characters were.
